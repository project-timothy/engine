# Ask Tim

Questions people ask about Tim, with short answers. You can also just ask Tim.

## Claude asks "Allow?" before everything Tim does. Can I stop that?

Yes, and it's the first thing to do. In the Claude app, open Customize, then Connectors, and tap Tim. Tim's tools come in two groups, Read-only and Write/delete: set each group to Always allow. When Tim learns something new, the app sets that one tool to ask again, so if the asking comes back, set the group again. It's safe: Tim never moves money, a yes over your ministry's dollar line still asks for your Face ID, and your own account is yours alone.

## How do I know what to do next?

Just say hi. Tim reads your books and tells you the one thing waiting on you, and about how long it takes: a yes, cash to count, a month ready to send, or a bank statement to send. Every answer ends with the next one, or with "Nothing's waiting on you."

Try: *"Hey Tim."*

## Will Tim keep explaining the same thing every time?

No. The first time something new comes up (a yes that takes Face ID, cash from a machine, a receipt in another currency, one that was partly yours, a drive from your phone, a month ready to send), Tim says what's happening, why, and the rule, once. After that it just does it. Ask why any time and Tim says it all again.

Try: *"Why does that take Face ID?"*

## How do I add Tim to my AI?

First tap the welcome link your ministry emailed you; it sets up Face ID or your fingerprint. Then, on your setup page, tap Add Tim to Claude: Claude opens with Tim's name and address filled in, so you tap Add, Connect, and Allow. Claude's free plan has room for one added connector like Tim. In ChatGPT (Plus or higher), copy Tim's link from the same page and add it under Settings, then Apps. When it asks you to sign in, use the same Face ID or fingerprint.

## Tim said my books are in the middle of an update. Did I lose anything?

No. Your books take one change at a time, so two changes can't collide. When yours arrives while another is being kept, Tim waits a few seconds for it to finish. If the morning's work on your books is still going, he asks you to try again in a minute, and nothing you sent is lost.

Try: *"Is it done now?"*

## I help with two ministries. Do I add Tim twice?

Yes. Each ministry's Tim has its own address, so each one is its own connector in Claude or ChatGPT. Your welcome email names the address for that ministry. Give each its own name in your AI so you can tell them apart, and ask the one whose books you mean.

Try: *"Which ministry is this Tim for?"*

## Claude says Tim is already there. What do I do?

Use the one you have. In Claude, open Customize, then Connectors, and tap Tim. If it says Connect, tap it and then Allow with your Face ID or fingerprint. Tim answers as whoever signs in there, whatever name the connector shows, so it's you once you've allowed it.

## Claude answered instead of Tim. How do I reach Tim?

Start with Tim's name, like "Tim, what's waiting for me?" Timothy or Timmy work too. If Claude still answers by itself, tap the + by the message box and check that Tim is switched on.

## Do I need a password?

No. Tim signs you in with Face ID, your fingerprint, or your computer's own sign-in. If your phone can't, Tim emails you a code. A welcome link that stopped working sends you a new one if you put in your email.

## What does the welcome page ask me?

Once Face ID is set up, a few questions, one at a time: whether to put your receipt page on your home screen, and, where your ministry offers it, whether to add Tim to Claude or ChatGPT. If your welcome came by email, Tim already knows its daily email reaches you and doesn't ask; otherwise it sends a hello for you to open. Mileage and Tim's dates in your calendar, where your ministry offers them, wait on the last screen until you want them. Nothing to type, no addresses and no codes. If your phone can't make a passkey, Use my email instead gets you in, since the welcome email already showed it's you. Without Face ID, Tim emails you a link each time you approve something over your ministry's dollar line or open a month, and each new phone or computer signs in by email. Try Face ID once more first; you can add it later from a new welcome link. The Face ID you just used covers each yes for ten minutes, on that phone only, so a yes is one tap; after that Tim asks for it again. Someone helping you on their own phone can't say yes for you. Not now is fine: open your welcome link again and tap Finish setting up.

## We have ten new missionaries. Do we set each one up by hand?

No. Give Tim one list, one person a line, in the words you'd use anyway: their name, what they do, where they serve, their email, and who looks after them. Tim checks the whole list first and says which line it can't read, so nothing is half done. Then each person gets their own welcome link by email, and their leader sees their reports from day one. The same list twice adds nobody twice. For now, whoever runs your Tim box gives Tim the list.

Try: *"Ivy Chen = missionary in Thailand, ivy.chen@example.org, looked after by Ann Lee"*

## Can Tim's dates show up in my own calendar?

Yes, where your ministry offers it. On your setup page's last screen, tap to set it up, say yes, and tap Subscribe when your phone asks. Things due that you're allowed to see, like a report or a visa renewal, then show up in the calendar you already use, with reminders ahead, and they update by themselves. Tim only adds dates. It never reads your calendar or anything in it. A lost phone? Your ministry can turn the old link off, and setup gives you a new one.

Try: *"What's coming due?"*

## How do I get Tim set up for my ministry?

Whoever is setting up Tim with you sends you a link. On your phone it asks six things: how support reaches you, the name on your IRS letter (with a picture of it), where you live, who looks over your spending and whether they also approve each month, and whether you drive. Then Tim says back what it heard and what it assumes, like your year running January to December. Say yes, and your books are set up next; you get an email from Tim when they're ready. If support comes straight to you, with no nonprofit of your own, Tim can't set that up by itself yet, says so, and keeps what you said.

## Do I have to type my nonprofit's name off my IRS letter?

Not where the person setting you up has switched it on. Take the picture of your letter, leave the name blank, and tap Continue. Tim reads the letter and says back your nonprofit's name and its Employer Identification Number. Tap Yes, that's right, and that's your answer. Tap No, and you type the name the way you always would. Tim keeps the picture either way. Where it isn't switched on, or Tim can't make out the letter, or it's a letter for a business rather than a nonprofit, you type the name.

Try: *"Take a picture of your IRS letter."*

## How do I add Tim to Claude or ChatGPT?

Setup does it with you, where your ministry offers it. In Claude, tap Add Tim to Claude: Claude opens its own add box with Tim's name and address already in it, and you tap Add, Connect, and Allow. Nothing to copy or type. In ChatGPT, it takes Plus or higher: copy Tim's link and add it under Settings, then Apps. Sign in with the same Face ID or fingerprint, then ask Tim anything in your own words. Setup counts it done the first time Tim answers you there.

Try: *"What's waiting for me?"*

## What's the receipt page on my home screen?

A one-tap way to send Tim a receipt: open it, take the photo, done. It lands with your month's receipts, the same as one you send through Tim. It only adds photos to your own receipts and never sees your money. A new phone, or a lost one? Your ministry can turn the old page off and give you a new one.

## Who am I signed in as?

Ask Tim. It says your name, what you do for the ministry, whose spending you look over, and who looks over yours.

Try: *"Who am I in Tim?"*

## What can Tim do for me?

Ask, and Tim answers for you: the receipts and reports you send if you file expenses, the months you look over if you review someone's. Tim keeps the books; you say what you want in plain words, and a yes when one is needed.

Try: *"What can Tim do for me?"*

## Can the other missionaries see my receipts?

No. You see your own. The people who look over your spending see your months, and so does whoever keeps the books, like your treasurer or your agency's finance office. Nobody else does. At a sending agency, member care sees who you are and your dates, like a visa renewal, and never your money. If your agency asks for it, every look through Tim is written down: who looked and whose it was.

Try: *"Who can see my expenses?"*

## How many emails will Tim send me?

At most one a morning, and only when something needs you: a month ready to look over, a yes waiting on you, or, on Fridays, drives to sort. When there's more than one, they come together in the same email, three things at most: if you look over several people's months, they're one list with a link each, and if people you look after are waiting on your yes, the email names them (never what or how much). Anything past three is a short list at the end. Emails never carry an amount; Tim or the link in the email shows that.

## Can I talk to Tim without Claude or ChatGPT?

Yes, where your ministry has switched on Tim's own voice. Open Tim's page on your phone and sign in once with Face ID or your fingerprint; your phone remembers you for 30 days. The one thing waiting on you sits at the top. Type in the box, or tap the microphone on your keyboard and say it, then tap Send. To add a receipt, tap Add a receipt photo and your camera opens; for a pile, tap Send several at once. Put the page on your home screen and it opens straight to Tim. Whoever looks over your month doesn't need it: their month page and their yes work the same as always.

Try: *"Hey Tim, what's waiting on me?"*

## Why does Tim ask me to tap Yes on his page?

Tim files what you tell him straight away: a receipt, a category, how much you paid. Anything that decides something, or an amount you didn't say yourself, waits for your tap. He shows one line under his message saying whose it is, what for, and how much, with Yes and Not now. Yes does exactly that, once; Not now leaves everything as it was. An approval over your ministry's line opens your own yes page instead, where Face ID or your fingerprint says it's you. A receipt or a bank line can never say yes for you, whatever it says.

Try: *"Send my September receipts to Linda."*

## Tim put up a Yes, but I asked him something else too. Does he still answer?

Yes. When something you said needs your tap, Tim tells you in his own words what the Yes would do and why, then answers the rest of what you asked, with the Yes under his message. One Yes waits at a time. If you'd rather not, tap Not now or just say something else: nothing changes until you tap Yes or type a plain yes, and Tim never says it's done while it's still waiting on you.

Try: *"Keep what SupportNet's 1099 says, and did I owe estimated tax this year?"*

## I tapped the button Tim gave me. Does he know it worked?

Yes. The next time you say something, Tim already knows what your tap did: that your receipt photo came in, that your yes went through, or that a button went unused. You never have to tell him twice. A receipt button you didn't use goes away once you say something new; ask, and Tim puts up a fresh one. A yes you asked for comes back once on its own.

Try: *"Ok, I sent the receipt."*

## My yes button ran out before I tapped it. Do I have to ask Tim all over again?

No. If you asked Tim to approve or decline something and your yes didn't go through, his next answer puts a fresh yes button under his message on its own, once, within 30 minutes of your ask. Nothing is decided until you tap it with Face ID or a fingerprint. If you've changed your mind, say so, like never mind, and the button stays away.

Try: *"It says the link expired."*

## Tim answered my question and nothing else. What if he saw something wrong in my books?

Tim answers what you asked and holds anything else he notices in your books for when you ask what's waiting, so one question never turns into another. When you're ready, ask what's waiting or ask him to look over the month, and he tells you.

Try: *"Anything else waiting on me this month?"*

## I just approved something by mistake. Can I take it back?

Yes, if it was your own yes or no and nothing else has happened in your books since. Tell Tim, like take back my yes on the August report, and it waits for a yes or no again, with your take-back kept on its record. Once anything else happens in your books, like a receipt you add or the morning's work, what you decided stands, and Tim tells you so.

Try: *"Oops, take back my yes on the August report."*

## Can I approve my own report, or does it have to go to someone else?

Ask Tim. He reads who decides each thing that's waiting, from your ministry's own setup, and tells you plainly. If the yes is yours, say approve and name it, like approve my September report: under your ministry's dollar line your words decide it, and over it Tim puts up the button to your own yes page. If someone else decides it, Tim says so and leaves it with them.

Try: *"Does my ministry let me approve my September report myself?"*

## I opened my receipt link a while after Tim sent it. Does it still work?

Yes, for as long as it's good for, even when Tim has been quiet in between: a receipt link works once within 15 minutes, and a catch-up link for a whole day. The same goes for the buttons on a month page you left open. After that, ask Tim for a new one.

Try: *"My receipt link stopped working. Can I have a new one?"*

## Several things are waiting. How do I tell Tim which one I mean?

Say what it is, the way you'd say it to a friend: who, what for, or how much, like my September report or the $64 printer cartridges. Tim names each thing the same way and never by a number, so there's nothing to look up or remember.

Try: *"Approve my September report, and leave the printer cartridges for now."*

## Can I just type yes on Tim's page instead of tapping?

Yes. Right after Tim shows Yes and Not now, typing a plain yes (yes, ok, yep that's right) does exactly what tapping Yes does, once. Anything more, like yes but make it $30, goes to Tim as words, and nothing runs until you tap or type a plain yes. For your own approval under your ministry's dollar line, say approve and name it, such as approve my August report or approve the $64 printer cartridges, and your words decide it, the same as when you talk to Tim through Claude. When only one thing waits on your yes, it's approved or approve it is enough. Over the line, or several at once, Tim puts up the button to your yes page, where Face ID or your fingerprint says it's you.

Try: *"Approve the $64 printer cartridges."*

## Does Tim read my receipt when I snap it?

Yes, where your ministry has switched it on. Snap the receipt and the page says back what it reads: who you paid, the day, and how much, in the money printed on it. Tap Yes, that's right, and it counts. Tap No and tell Tim how much it was. If you already told Tim the amount and the receipt shows a different one, the page asks which is right, and nothing counts until you tap. Where it isn't switched on, or Tim can't make out the receipt, the page works the way it always has.

Try: *"Here's a receipt from the market."*

## Why does Tim take a little longer on some questions?

Some questions need more care: taxes, a bank statement, getting set up, or something you've already asked once. For those, Tim works it through more carefully on his own, so the answer can take a few extra seconds. You never need to ask for it.

Try: *"What tax bracket am I in this year?"*

## What happens when Tim reaches his limit for the month?

Each ministry has a monthly limit on how much Tim can talk, so the bill never surprises anyone. When he's getting close, he says so once a day. If he reaches it, he tells you, and he's back on the first of the month. Your receipt photos, your receipt page, a Yes already waiting for your tap, and your month page keep working the whole time.

## How do I send a receipt?

Tell Tim what you paid and what for. Tim sends a link; tap it and the page opens your camera. Snap the receipt and you're done. Tim keeps the amount you said, in the currency you paid.

Try: *"Here's a receipt, 4,300 rupees for the bus to Pokhara."*

## I said the wrong amount. How do I fix it?

Just say the right one. If it was a different receipt, say which ("the bus ticket").

Try: *"That last receipt was 1,250 rupees."*

## How do I put a receipt under the right category?

Say it in your own words. Tim knows the ministry's list, like Travel, Meals and Supplies, and puts it there. If you look over someone's month, you can move their receipt too, and Tim shows the change before you say yes.

Try: *"That bus ticket goes under Travel."*

## I paid in rupees. How does that turn into dollars?

Tim keeps what you paid as you said it, and works out its dollar value at the exchange rate on the receipt's own day. Both show on your month, with the rate.

Try: *"Show me my October receipts."*

## What's in my month so far?

Ask Tim for the month. You'll see each receipt's day and dollars, any drives, any bank lines, the total, and where the review stands.

Try: *"Show me my September receipts."*

## Which fund does my receipt go to?

Your ministry's. If it keeps more than one fund, each line on your month says which; with one, Tim doesn't repeat it on every line.

## Why doesn't Tim read me codes like "events#17"?

Those are Tim's own bookmarks for a receipt. Your AI uses them behind the scenes and tells you about a receipt by its day, its amount, and who was paid.

## How do my ministry drives get paid back?

The tracker app on your phone sends Tim the miles and times, never where you went. On Fridays Tim asks what the week's drives were for. A ministry drive goes on your month with your receipts, priced at that day's mileage rate ("Oct 12: 14 miles, visiting the church in Pokhara, $10.64 at 76 cents a mile"), and your reviewer approves it with the rest. A drive you call personal never turns into money. No tracker yet? Open your welcome link, tap Finish setting up, and set up mileage from the last screen.

Try: *"Tuesday's drive was the church visit in Pokhara."*

## What if my phone stops reporting partway through a drive?

The miles still count, most of them. Phones, iPhones especially, sometimes go quiet for a while in the middle of a drive. Tim holds the drive open for up to 30 minutes, and when your phone speaks again farther down the road, the stretch it missed counts as a straight line, a little short of the road you drove. A phone that stays quiet longer than that ends the drive where Tim last heard you moving, and Tim never keeps where that was.

## I'm behind on receipts. How do I catch up?

Ask Tim for a catch-up link. Tap it, choose every receipt at once (photos from your camera roll, PDFs, or the statements your bank's website exports), and send. The link works for a day, so you can send more as you find them. Your reviewer can ask for the link for you, too.

Try: *"I've got a year of receipts to catch up on."*

## Can I see what I picked before it sends?

Yes. On a catch-up link, the page lists every file you chose by name. Tap "Send these" when it's right, or "Choose again" if a personal photo slipped in.

## My statements are in iCloud Drive. How do I pick them?

Tap the button on your catch-up page, choose Choose File, then type the folder's name in the search box at the top. Select the files and tap Open.

## I can't find my CSV file in the iPhone's file picker.

On your catch-up page, tap the button and choose Choose File, then switch the picker's view to List. Your CSV files are easy to spot that way. Tap the ones you want, then tap Open.

Try: *"Send me a catch-up link."*

## My link says it expired or didn't go through.

Ask Tim for a new one; it takes a second. A camera link lasts a short while and a catch-up link lasts a day. A link also stops working if Tim's box restarts, so if a fresh link fails, ask once more.

Try: *"Send me a new catch-up link."*

## How does a receipt from March end up in March?

Each receipt goes in its own month, by the day on the receipt, not the day you sent it. Your Claude reads the day off it, or you say it. A receipt in another currency is turned into dollars at that day's rate.

Try: *"That one's from March 5."*

## Who reads my receipts?

Your own Claude. Tim hands it the receipts you sent; it reads the day, the amount, and who you paid, and walks you through them. An amount counts only once you say it's right.

Try: *"Read my receipts."*

## Tim asked me what day I paid for my train ticket. Why?

A train, bus or ferry ticket often prints only the day you travel, and that isn't always the day you paid. So when a receipt shows no day it was paid, Tim reads the rest and asks you the day. Tell him, and it goes in that day's month.

Try: *"I paid for the train ticket on October 2."*

## Do I have to say yes to every receipt one at a time?

No. Tim walks you through a month ("July: 14 receipts, $612.40, here's what I read"). Fix anything that's off in plain words, then one yes covers the rest. Then say "send July for review" and your reviewer gets the whole month to say yes to at once.

Try: *"Yes, July's right. Send it for review."*

## My month was approved, then I found another receipt. Does it all need approving again?

No. The new receipt waits for a yes of its own, with a line saying what's already approved ("Already approved Oct 13: 2 receipts, $42.31"). What was approved stays approved. If an approved receipt changes, only that one comes back for a yes.

## Where's my year?

Ask Tim. You'll see what each month spent and what of it is owed back to you (only what you paid yourself), what's still waiting on you, where each month's review stands, and your totals by category: your receipts, your school payments, card purchases from the statement, and cash you said went to the ministry.

Try: *"Where's my year?"*

## Can I see our books for the month, the quarter, or the year?

Yes, any time, in whatever span you say: September, last quarter, 2025, or year to date. Tim says what came in and what went out by kind, each month of it, what's owed back to people and what's been paid back, what's still waiting on someone, cash in hand at the end, and where each budget stands as of then. Everyone sees the books for the people their role looks after: a missionary their own, a team leader their team, whoever runs the money the whole ministry. The numbers come from the same lines as each month's report, so the two always agree.

Try: *"How do the books look for last quarter?"*

## How much is in the ministry's bank account?

Ask Tim for the books, for any span. When the ministry's bank statement carries a balance (most banks' exports do, Chase's among them), Tim keeps the balance at the end of each day and checks the books against it: what was there before the first line, plus every line since. If the two agree, Tim says how much is in the bank. If they don't, Tim names the days and the dollars between them, which usually means a statement was never sent, and asks for the one that covers those days. Sending a statement again only adds what's missing. Your own account's balance is never kept.

Try: *"How much is in the bank?"*

## Are Tim's books real double-entry books, the kind an accountant expects?

Yes. Every line Tim keeps (a gift, a bank payment, a receipt you paid for, ATM cash and what it bought, money sent to you, a payback) is written as a balanced entry: what came in or went out on one side, where the money sat on the other. Each person has their own running account, so what the ministry owes you and what you hold of the ministry's show plainly. Ask for the books and Tim says whether they balance and whether the bank account in them matches what the bank says. Code writes every entry from the lines you already gave Tim; nothing is typed twice.

Try: *"Do our books balance for 2026?"*

## Can Tim give our board its financial statements?

Yes, for any month, quarter or year. Ask for the board's statements and Tim gives the two a board reads: what came in and what was spent by kind, with the change in net assets; and what the ministry has at the end (the bank, cash in hand, ministry money someone holds) and what it owes, with net assets. Once you've said what share of your spending is program, every kind of spending is split program and management too. Tim also makes a page and a PDF for board members who don't use an AI. Every figure is the books' own, so it always matches, and Tim says whether it balances and agrees with the bank.

Try: *"Show me the board's statements for 2026."*

## Does the board get the statements each month without asking?

Yes. Once a month's receipts have their yes, everyone who looks over every month gets one email with that month's statements attached as a PDF, so anyone can read them on a phone with no AI and no sign-in. It comes once a month, the email itself says no figures, and the PDF is the same board pack Tim gives when you ask. Only people your ministry gave an email address get it.

Try: *"Show me October's board pack."*

## Can our accountant see the books without using Tim?

Yes. Ask Tim for a year's books for your accountant and you get plain files: every entry as text in the Beancount format (any text editor opens it, and accounting tools can read it), the trial balance as a spreadsheet, and the numbers your 990 and 1099s start from, like gross receipts and what was spent on pay. The year opens from what was brought forward, so it stands on its own. Before handing them over, Tim reads the files back and checks they balance and match the books to the cent. Nothing about your books is locked inside Tim.

Try: *"Get the 2026 books ready for our CPA."*

## How does Tim split our spending into program and management?

By the share you say once: "95 percent of what we spend is program." Whoever looks over every month sets it, and the newest one said stands. Each kind of spending is split by it to the cent, so the two parts always add up to what was spent.

Try: *"95 percent of what we spend is program."*

## I paid a ministry bill out of my own pocket. How do I get it on the books?

Tell Tim what it was, the day, and how much: "I paid the state filing fee, $312.50, on November 12." Anyone at the ministry can, a board member as much as the missionary. Your ministry's own rules say who gives the yes: at a solo ministry, like a missionary's own nonprofit, your own yes does it; at a church or a ministry organization someone else who approves spending says yes, never the one who paid. Once it's approved, the books count it as the ministry's spending and show it as owed back to you. When the ministry pays you back, tell Tim which bank line did it, and what you're owed comes down by that much.

Try: *"I paid the $312.50 filing fee myself on November 12."*

## How do the books close for the year?

Once a year is over and the 1099 and 990 numbers are taken from it, whoever looks over every month says "close the 2026 books." Tim reads back what came in and what was spent, by kind, and any month still waiting on a yes. Your yes, by Face ID or the emailed link, closes it. Months stay open until then. After that, nothing dated in that year changes: a receipt that turns up late can go in this year's books instead, with its own day kept in your words, and a statement line from the closed year is left out.

Try: *"Close the 2026 books."*

## Can a closed year be reopened?

Yes. Whoever looks over every month says "reopen the 2026 books." Tim first warns that reopening may change the numbers last year's 1099s and 990 were taken from, so anything already filed may need correcting. At a solo ministry, the missionary's own yes reopens it, by Face ID or the emailed link. At a church, the bookkeeper asks and a supervisor says yes. At an organization, accounting asks and its supervisor says yes. The one who asks never gives the yes there. Once it's open, changes dated in that year go in again, and you can close it again the same way.

Try: *"Reopen the 2026 books."*

## Can I send my bank statements?

Yes. Download the CSV file from your bank's website and send it through your catch-up link. Tim asks whose account it is. The ministry's account counts in full.

Try: *"Here are my bank statements."*

## I sent two statements. Do I have to tell Tim which is which?

Not when their names already say it. Name the files the way you'd say them, like Ministry checking and My own checking, and Tim asks one question: the ministry's and yours, right? Your yes settles both. If a name doesn't say, or Tim read it wrong, just tell him whose each one is.

Try: *"Yes, the ministry's and mine."*

## My bank won't give me a CSV file. Can I send a photo or a PDF of my statement?

Yes, where your ministry has switched it on. Send the photo or the PDF through your catch-up link, like anything else. Tim reads it and checks the arithmetic itself: the starting balance plus every line has to come to the ending balance the statement prints, to the cent, with every line inside the statement's dates. If it does, the page says how many lines, the dates, and the two balances, and one tap of Keep them keeps them. Then Tim asks whose account it is, the same as for a CSV file. If the lines don't add up, Tim keeps none of it and asks for the CSV file instead. Where it isn't switched on, a photo goes in with your receipts.

Try: *"Here's a picture of my bank statement."*

## Tim wouldn't take my bank statement. Why?

Tim only keeps a statement it can read whole, so no line is left out or counted wrong. It says which day or amount stopped it. Download the file again with dates written month first (08/25/2026) or as 2026-08-25, amounts like 1,234.56, and a minus sign on money going out (or separate Debit and Credit columns).

## Will Tim see my personal spending?

Only to help you pick. From your own account, Tim keeps the lines you say were the ministry's and nothing else; the rest is never saved. Your reviewer never sees your own account.

Try: *"The taxi and the supermarket were ministry; the rest is mine."*

## Tim flagged some lines on my own statement. Do I have to name each one?

No. Say "keep what you flagged" and Tim keeps the lines he flagged for you, and nothing else from your own account. Add any he missed in the same breath, like the supermarket. Tim only keeps lines he showed you, and the rest are never saved.

Try: *"Keep what you flagged, and the supermarket too."*

## What about money I sent through Remitly?

It's money moving, not spending. Your receipts and your school list show where it went. Money the ministry paid you back isn't spending either.

## I take cash out at an ATM and spend rupees. How does that count?

The withdrawal is cash in hand, not spending. Tell Tim how many rupees it gave (the slip shows it; several at once is fine, like "Feb 14 gave 26,500, Apr 11 gave 19,900"), and your rupee receipts and scholarships count at what that cash really cost: the ministry's cash first, oldest first, then any of your own. When both pay for something, only your part is owed back to you. The ATM fee counts on its own. Tim tells you how much cash is still in hand.

Try: *"That ATM withdrawal gave 40,000 rupees."*

## My pay from a support platform lands in the ministry's account. Is that the ministry's money?

## How do missionaries get paid, and what does Tim keep track of?

Most missionaries are paid by one or more of three kinds of payer. A support platform, like EquipNet or a mission base's account, collects gifts given for you and pays them to you; everything it pays you is on a 1099, the IRS form for pay to someone who isn't an employee. A sending church pays you a set amount on a 1099 and pays back your ministry costs against receipts, apart from your pay. An agency either employs you, and sends a W-2 like any employer, or pays you on a 1099. Money a friend or relative gives you personally is a gift. Tim keeps it on the record and off your taxes. Tell Tim who pays you once. When a deposit that's your own money lands, Tim matches it to its payer by the name the bank prints on it, and asks when it can't tell. In January, tell Tim what each payer's tax form says the day you open it, even if it looks wrong: Tim keeps it as printed and checks it against what it saw arrive, so a payment that went to an account Tim doesn't see shows up as the difference, and a form that says too little is named so you can ask for a corrected one. For a W-2, give box 1, your wages, and box 2, the federal income tax withheld; wages never go on Schedule C. The packet also works out your self-employment tax, the Social Security and Medicare you pay both halves of, which the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion never lowers; drafts your days abroad from the days your bank shows you spending in another currency, for you to check against your travel dates; and works your federal tax out to your top bracket, with next year's four estimated payments. When you pay a ministry cost from your own pay and no one pays you back, pick it from your own statement and tell Tim it came from your pay, or, for cash, tell Tim the same about its receipt: the ministry owes you nothing for it, and it comes off your 1099 income on the packet. The packet comes as one page and a PDF to hand to whoever does your taxes.

Try: *"EquipNet pays me; it's a support platform. What's in my 2026 tax packet?"*

## My 1099 says a different amount than I was paid. Should I still tell Tim what it says?

Yes. Tell Tim the number printed on the form, even when you know it's wrong. Tim asks for your yes with a line like "Keep what SupportNet's 2026 tax form says: $200.00, to check against what came in." That keeps the form's figure beside the deposits Tim saw arrive, and your deposits stay as they are. Your tax packet then names the difference: a form that says more than Tim saw means some went to an account Tim doesn't see, and a form that says less is flagged so you can check it and ask the payer for a corrected one before you file. When the corrected form comes, tell Tim what it says, and the newest word stands.

Try: *"SupportNet's 1099 says $200, but they paid me $250 in September."*

## Someone sends me money every month. Is it a gift or pay?

It depends on why they send it. Money a friend or relative gives you because they love you is a gift: Tim keeps it on the record and off your taxes. Money sent because of your ministry work is income, even with no tax form, and it goes on Schedule C. When a payer you called a personal gift has a name that sounds like a church, a fund, or another organization, or sends money about every month, Tim asks you once: "Do they send this because of your ministry work?" Your answer stays with that payer. A one-time gift, like your mom's birthday check, needs no payer: tell Tim who gave it on that one deposit, and it stays a gift without counting anything else she sends.

Try: *"The $500 from my mom on October 1 was her birthday gift to me."*

## Someone sends me money every month, partly for my ministry work and partly as a gift. How does Tim count it?

When Tim asks whether they send it because of your ministry work, answer "some of each." Tim asks how much of each payment is for your ministry work: say it in dollars, like $150 of each $500, or as a percentage, like 30%. Tim works out the other part and says both back with their percentages. Every deposit from them is split the same way, to the cent: the part for your ministry work counts as income on Schedule C of your tax packet, and the rest stays a personal gift, kept off your taxes. When the cents don't divide evenly, the ministry part rounds down and the gift gets the extra cent, so the two always add up to the deposit. If it changes, tell Tim the new split, or say yes or no to make it all one kind again. Anyone who sends all pay or all gift is counted exactly as before.

Try: *"My dad sends $500 a month. $150 of it is for my ministry work and the rest is a gift."*

## What does Tim need from me for my taxes, and who sees it?

A few things only you know. Your filing status: Tim works out single, or married filing jointly, and leaves any other status to your preparer. If you file jointly, your spouse's wages and the federal tax withheld from them, the two numbers from boxes 1 and 2 of their W-2, said together. Last year's total tax, from line 24 of last year's Form 1040, which sets the smallest estimates that keep you clear of a penalty. And each estimated payment you've made, with the day you paid it. Tell Tim each one once, in your own words; the newest word stands. Whoever looks over every month at your ministry can tell Tim these for you and read your tax packet, since they keep your books with you; no one else at your ministry sees them. To let someone else read your packet, like your spouse or another board member on your ministry's list, tell Tim to share it with them, and tell Tim when to stop. Your preparer gets the PDF from you.

Try: *"I'm married filing jointly; my wife's W-2 says $41,200 in wages and $3,100 withheld."*

## Why does Tim ask for my travel dates?

For the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion, which lets someone living abroad leave up to $132,900 of 2026 pay off their income tax. The common way to qualify is the physical presence test: 330 full days outside the United States in any 12 months in a row. Only you know your travel dates, so tell Tim each trip: the day you left the United States, the day you came back, and the country, like "I left for Nepal on January 8 and came home June 2." Still away? Leave the day back out. Tim counts full days only, so the day you left and the day you came back don't count, and finds your best 12 months in a row. A trip still going counts through the end of those 12 months, and your packet says how many of the days are still ahead. Your bank is the cross-check: Tim names any day it shows you spending in another currency on a day you said you were in the United States. Said a trip wrong? Say it again with the right dates, or tell Tim to forget it.

Try: *"I left for Nepal on January 8 and came home on June 2."*

## What tax bracket am I in, and what will I owe?

Ask Tim for your tax packet. It works your federal tax out the way the return does: your Schedule C profit, self-employment tax, your wages and your spouse's if you file jointly, the standard deduction, the qualified business income deduction on work done in the United States, your taxable income, your income tax, and your top bracket, the rate on your last dollar of taxable income. When nothing is taxable after the standard deduction, there's no bracket and no income tax, and self-employment tax is all you owe. Asked before the year ends, it works on the year so far and says so. If your travel dates show 330 full days abroad in 12 months, the packet shows your tax two ways, with the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion and without it, and leaves the choice to you and your preparer: taking it gives up the refundable part of the child tax credit and the earned income credit, which can be worth more to a family. Federal tax only. It's a planning estimate for you and your preparer, and nothing on it is filed.

Try: *"What tax bracket am I in this year?"*

## How much should I pay in estimated tax, and when?

Your tax packet sizes next year's four estimated payments, due on or about April 15, June 15, September 15, and the next January 15. Each is a quarter of 90% of this year's total federal tax, less the tax withheld from wages, assuming next year looks like this one; none are needed when you'd owe under $1,000. If your income changes, ask again. Tell Tim each payment you make, with the day, and the packet shows it as already paid against that year. Tell Tim last year's total tax too: when it's smaller, paying it in four parts keeps you clear of a penalty (110% of it when last year's adjusted gross income passed $150,000).

Try: *"How much are my quarterly estimates for next year?"*

## What doesn't Tim work out on my taxes?

Your packet ends with a short list for your preparer. Tim leaves out state income tax, itemized deductions, and credits like the child tax credit, the earned income credit, and health insurance credits, which lower what you owe. It leaves out a minister's housing allowance and Form 4361, the opt-out from self-employment tax; the bona fide residence test (Tim works out the physical presence test only); the foreign tax credit and the foreign housing exclusion; a spouse's self-employment; and retirement contributions. Tim works out single and married filing jointly only. Hand your preparer the PDF and they add the rest.

Try: *"What will my preparer still need to add to my packet?"*

## Our church pays mileage back at less than the IRS rate. What happens to the difference?
