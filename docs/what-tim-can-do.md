## Ask Tim

- Who you are with Tim: your name, what you do, whose spending you look over and who looks over yours. And how to do anything with Tim, in plain answers. *"Who am I in Tim, and how do I send a receipt?"*
- What is coming due, and how many days are left. *"What's coming due this month?"*
- What is waiting for someone's yes or no. *"Is anything waiting on me?"*
- Which bills are still unpaid, the soonest first, and the total. *"What bills do we still owe?"*
- Find a bill by who sent it or its number. *"Find the bill from the water company."*
- Whether this morning's work ran, and how it went. *"Did this morning's work run?"*
- Where the month's closing stands. *"Where does closing September stand?"*
- Your expense reports, their totals, and whether you have been paid back. *"Have I been paid back for August?"*
- A month of receipts in dollars. A receipt paid in another currency shows what you paid and its dollar value on that day. *"Show me my October receipts."*
- Where your year stands: each month's receipts and total, what's still waiting, and totals by category. Once your ministry sets a budget, what's spent and what's left of each one. *"Where's my year? How much is left for scholarships?"*
- Your ministry's books for any span, any time: a month, a quarter, a year, or year to date. What came in and what went out by kind, month by month, what's owed back, what's still waiting, and where each budget stands. You see the people your role looks after. *"How do the books look for last quarter?"*
- Your ministry's statements the way a board reads them: what came in and what was spent by kind, what the ministry has and owes at the end, net assets, and the program and management split, for any month, quarter or year. *"Show me the board's statements for 2026."*
- Your own Claude reads the receipts you sent in a pile: the day, the amount, and who you paid. *"Read my receipts."*
- Before you buy something, what your ministry's own rules say about it, with the handbook and page. *"Can I buy a $1,400 projector for the school?"*
- A year's books for your accountant, in plain files any accountant can open: every entry as text, the trial balance as a spreadsheet, and the numbers your 990 and 1099s start from. They balance and match Tim's books, checked when they're made. *"Get the 2026 books ready for our CPA."*
- Your donors for a year: who gave, each gift and its day, each donor's total, and the letters each one is owed: a written acknowledgment of each gift of $250 or more and a year-end giving statement, or a thank-you for a grant from a donor-advised fund. *"Who gave to us in 2026, and what letters do they need?"*
- Your tax packet for a year: what each payer paid you that Tim saw arrive, checked against what its tax form says, with personal gifts kept off it; your self-employment tax; your full days abroad, counted from the travel dates you told Tim and checked against your bank; and next year's four estimate dates. *"Show me my 2026 tax packet."*
- When your handbook changes, which of your rules no longer read word for word in it, and its pages to draft from. *"What changed in our handbook?"*
- Says again what Tim explained the first time something new came up: what's happening, why, and the rule. *"Why does that take Face ID?"*

## Tell Tim

- Say yes or no to something waiting on you. Under your ministry's limit, just say so; over it, Tim asks you to confirm with Face ID, or emails you a link. *"Approve September's report."*
- Add a receipt: say what you paid, then snap the photo from the link Tim sends. *"Here's a receipt, 4,300 rupees for the bus to Pokhara."*
- Put a receipt under a category, like Travel or Meals. *"That bus ticket goes under Travel."*
- Say how much a receipt was, after you've snapped it. *"That last receipt was 1,250 rupees."*
- Say what a drive was for. Tim keeps the miles and times your phone tracked, never where you went. *"Tuesday's drive was the church visit in Pokhara."*
- Catching up? One link takes a whole pile at once (receipt photos, PDFs, your bank's statements, a school list) and works for a day. *"I've got a year of receipts to catch up on."*
- Say which day a receipt is from, so it lands in its own month at that day's rate. *"That one's from March 5."*
- What a receipt reads is kept as a reading. The amount counts once you agree to it. *"Read the next receipt and tell me what it says."*
- One yes for a month of receipts, once Tim has walked you through what it read. *"Yes, September's right."*
- Send a month to your reviewer for their yes, all of it at once, when it's ready. *"Send September for review."*
- Send the statements your bank's website exports: the ministry's account counts in full, and from your own account Tim keeps only the lines you pick. PayPal's export, or a giving app's, brings in each gift with its donor named. *"Here are my bank statements."*
- Pick which lines of your own account were the ministry's. The rest never stay with Tim. *"The taxi and the supermarket were ministry; the rest is mine."*
- Send your list of scholarships and school fees, and each one counts in its own month with no receipt needed. *"Here's my scholarship list."*
- Say what a bank line was for when you have no receipt for it, or how many rupees an ATM gave. *"The guest house was the team's hotel in Kathmandu."*
- Take part of a bank line, cash you spent, or a receipt out as something else, in your own words. The rest stays where it was. *"1,300 rupees of that was mine."*
- Tell Tim how much cash you have in hand. He checks it against the books and asks about anything missing. *"I've got 9,000 rupees left."*
- Say where cash with no receipt went: ministry spending like tea and taxis, or your own use. *"1,500 rupees were tea and taxis for the team."*
- Say which of two close receipts a bank line paid, when Tim asks. *"It was the 4,300 one."*
- Asks for the OK your ministry's rules say to get before you buy, from whoever the rule names, and tells you where it stands. *"Can you ask for an OK on a $300 printer?"*
- Sets your ministry's budget for the year, one kind of spending at a time, in your own words. *"Our scholarship budget this year is $12,000."*
- Closes the books for a year that's over, once the 1099 and 990 numbers are taken. After your yes, nothing dated in that year changes. *"Close the 2026 books."*
- Records a ministry expense you paid from your own pocket, like a state filing fee. At a solo ministry your own yes approves it; at a church or an organization someone else says yes. Then the books show it as owed back to you until the bank pays you back. *"I paid the state's $312.50 filing fee myself on November 12."*
- Sets what share of the ministry's spending is its program, the rest management, so the statements split every kind of spending by it. *"95 percent of what we spend is program."*
- Keeps what your donor letters need that the books don't hold: the ministry's EIN and mailing address, who signs and their title, and a donor's mailing address. You say each once. *"Our EIN is 12-3456789, and Linda Park signs as Treasurer."*
- Keeps who pays you: a support platform like EquipNet, a sending church, an agency, or a personal gift; in January what each one's tax form says, a W-2's wages and tax withheld too; and whether a gift comes because of your ministry work. *"EquipNet pays me; it's a support platform."*
- Reopens a closed year, after a warning that it may change the numbers last year's 1099s and 990 came from. At a solo ministry your own yes reopens it; at a church or an organization someone else who approves for the books says yes. *"Reopen the 2026 books."*
- Drafts a new rule from your newest handbook, quoting it word for word; each draft waits for your yes, and the old rule counts until then. *"Draft the new rules from our handbook."*
- Drafts the board's yearly pay resolution for a missionary; it waits for the yes of a director with no family tie, and Tim never sends the money. *"The board set Ruth's pay for 2027 at $24,000, paid on the 1st."*
- Keeps what only you know for your taxes: single or married filing jointly, your spouse's wages and tax withheld, last year's total tax, each estimated payment you made, and who else may read your tax packet. *"I'm married filing jointly; my wife's W-2 says $41,200 in wages and $3,100 withheld."*
- Keeps each trip you took: the day you left the United States, the day you came back, and the country. Your days abroad are counted from these. *"I left for Nepal on January 8 and came home on June 2."*

## What Tim does on its own

- Reads the bills that come in, files them, and asks a person before anything is paid.
- Notices money on its way in and checks it off when the bank shows it arrived.
- Sends each person one short page a week that leads with what needs them.
- Gets the month ready to close and seals it once a person says yes.
- Remembers the dates that matter, like filings, renewals, passports and visas, and reminds you weeks ahead.
- Turns receipts into an expense report and follows it until the money is paid back.
- Collects documents from the ministry's mailbox for the other lanes.
- Turns the ministry's handbook into rules, and asks a person to say yes to each one before it counts.
- Notices when the list of projects or funds drifts out of step with the books.
- Sends each decision to the right person, emails them when it waits (at most once a day), tells a reviewer when a month is ready, and covers for someone who is away.
- Files the hours people report.

## What Tim asks when you start

1. What kind of work will Tim keep the books for?
2. What is the legal name, the one on your tax filings?
3. And what's your name and email? Like: Pat Lee, pat@example.org
4. What do you do there?
5. What time zone are you in?
6. What month does your financial year start?
7. How is the business set up for taxes?
8. Is it a church, or another kind of charity?
9. How does the work get paid for?
10. Does anyone look over your spending, like a sponsor, a board member or an accountant? Their name and email, like: Dale Ortiz, dale@example.org. Or leave it blank.
11. Should they also approve each month, or just look it over?
12. Up to what amount can someone okay their own bill? Above it, a second person does.
13. Above what amount should a bill need two people to okay it?
14. Up to what amount can someone okay a request just by telling their AI? Above it, they confirm on their phone with Face ID.
15. Workers sometimes carry the ministry's cash, like money from an ATM abroad. Up to how much may one person hold before a director okays more?
16. When someone drives for the ministry, should Tim pay it back at the IRS rate for that year, or at a rate the ministry sets? Say IRS rate, or the ministry's rate in cents a mile, like 65.
17. Who else is involved, and what do they do? One per line, like: Sam Lee = board
18. Does anyone look after other people's spending, like a regional director over the missionaries in her region? One per line, like: Ann Lee = Sam Ortiz, Mei Tan
19. Any words Tim should never use in what it writes for you, like 'missionary' for work in a sensitive country? Leave it blank if there are none.
20. American or British spelling?
